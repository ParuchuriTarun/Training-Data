Use who when the pronoun is the subject of a verb, and whom when it is the object of a verb or a preposition. A reliable test is to substitute he or him: if he fits, use who; if him fits, use whom.

When should I use a semicolon instead of a dash? They both seem to connect ideas.
@@@ ASSISTANT
A semicolon joins two independent clauses that are closely related and roughly equal in importance: The deadline is Friday; the client needs the files by noon. Corrected: We need three supplies: paper, ink, and staples.

Use dashes sparingly in formal prose. One well-placed dash can emphasize a contrast; several in a paragraph make the writing feel breathless. Use a semicolon when the relationship is calm and logical, and a dash when you deliberately want emphasis or interruption.

@@@ ASSISTANT

What exactly is parallel structure, and how do I fix a sentence that is not parallel?
@@@ ASSISTANT
Parallel structure means that items joined by a list, a comparison, or a correlative pair use the same grammatical form. If the first item is a gerund, the others should be gerunds; if one is an infinitive, the others should be infinitives.

What is the difference between connotation and denotation? I want my word choice to be precise, not just technically correct.
@@@ ASSISTANT
Denotation is a word's literal, dictionary meaning. Connotation is the set of associations, feelings, and evaluations the word carries. Two words can share a denotation while differing sharply in connotation.

Words also carry associations that vary by culture, region, and generation, so a dictionary alone cannot settle every choice.

To edit for connotation, identify the attitude you want readers to take toward the subject, then check whether each loaded word supports that attitude. Do not use a euphemism to obscure a real problem, and do not use a harsh word when a neutral one is more accurate.

How do I read an academic article actively and then summarize it without copying the author's sentences?
@@@ ASSISTANT
Active reading is a deliberate conversation with the text. Preview the title, abstract, headings, and conclusion first, then turn the headings into questions. As you read, annotate the author's central claim, the evidence for it, key definitions, and any limitations. After each section, close the article and say the main point in your own words. That retrieval step exposes whether you understood the argument or merely recognized the words.

A good summary is shorter than the source, accurate, objective, and focused on the main claim and its essential support. It should not add your opinion unless the assignment asks for evaluation. It should also not be a patchwork of copied phrases. If you use an exact phrase, put it in quotation marks and cite it.

Weak summary: The article is about remote work and says it is good. This is vague and evaluative; it does not identify the author's actual finding.

Better: Chen (2024) finds that remote workers value flexible schedules more than office amenities, but nearly half report difficulty disconnecting after hours. The summary names the author, the year, and the two-part finding. It preserves the article's tension instead of flattening it into praise.

To avoid accidental copying, take notes in bullet points without looking at the original sentences, then write the summary from those notes. Compare it with the source afterward. Check that names, numbers, and qualifications are correct. Finally, add a citation in the required style.

My thesis statements always feel either obvious or too broad. What makes a thesis arguable and manageable?
@@@ ASSISTANT
A strong thesis makes a debatable claim, narrow enough to support within the assignment, and specific enough to preview the reasoning. It is not a fact, a question, or an announcement of what the paper will do.

Weak: This paper will discuss the effects of remote work. This is an announcement; it takes no position. Corrected: Remote work improves retention for experienced employees, but only when managers set clear boundaries around after-hours communication. The revision makes a claim, limits the population, and identifies a condition.

Weak: Technology affects education. This is arguable in the loosest sense but too broad for a short paper. Corrected: Requiring students to submit handwritten drafts in one first-year writing course reduced editing errors and increased revision time. Now the claim is specific enough to test with evidence from that course.

A useful thesis often has a main claim and a because-clause or a qualifying condition. That structure shows readers what the paper must prove. It should also be placed where readers expect it, usually at the end of the introduction, though the exact position can vary.

Test your thesis with three questions. Can a reasonable person disagree? If no, it is probably a fact. Can you support it with the evidence available in the assigned length? If not, narrow it. Does it tell the reader what the body paragraphs will establish? If not, make the reasoning more explicit.

Practice: write a thesis for an essay on whether cities should ban private cars downtown.

Answer: Cities should restrict private cars in downtown areas because the policy reduces air pollution, supports public transit, and makes streets safer for pedestrians. A different position would need a different causal argument, not just a different opinion.

I need help with quotation and citation mechanics. When do I use quotation marks, and how do I integrate a quote without just dropping it into a paragraph?
@@@ ASSISTANT
Use quotation marks when you reproduce someone else's exact words. Quote sparingly, when the wording itself matters: a memorable phrase, a precise definition, or a claim you will analyze closely. Paraphrase when you only need the idea, but still cite the source. Summaries and paraphrases require citations too, even though they do not take quotation marks.

A quotation should be integrated into your own sentence. Weak: "The policy failed." A bare quotation dropped into a paragraph has no author, context, or page, so readers cannot tell whose words it is or why it matters. Corrected: In her analysis of the program, Rivera concludes that the policy "failed to address the root causes of absenteeism" (14). The signal phrase names the author, the sentence frames the quoted claim, and the parenthetical citation gives the location in MLA style.

Copy the quotation exactly. Use ellipses for omitted words, brackets for changes needed for grammar, and sic only for an error you must preserve. Do not silently alter punctuation or wording. For long quotations, follow your style guide's block-quote threshold: MLA uses more than four lines of prose, while APA uses forty or more words. A block quote is indented and usually has no quotation marks.

Punctuation placement depends on the style. In MLA, the period normally follows the parenthetical citation. In APA, the period also follows the citation, while a question mark that belongs to the quotation may come before it. Follow the assigned guide consistently.
